## 1. Read this first

**Finance is not guesswork.** The finance questions are taken from the notes
sentence by sentence. Read `2-lectures/0-COURSE-NOTES-2025.pdf` pages 11 to 24
and `2-lectures/10-financing-new-ventures.pdf` slides 30 to 64 as sentences, not
as topics. They are the answer key.

**The option letters change every year. The answer text does not.**
Example: "A market segment on which a business can choose to concentrate its
efforts" was `c` in 2005, `c` in 2007, `a` in one 2020 item and `e` in another.
The answer was always **market niche**.

> **Rule 1: Learn the answer text. Never learn the letter.**

**The code for the i/ii/iii section also changes.** See section 2. Read the key
on the paper before you answer. Students lose marks here every year.

> **Rule 2: Read the KEY box on the paper first. Circle it.**

### Exam format

| Section | Content | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A | i / ii / iii multiple completion | Uses a code, A to E. The code changes. |
| B | "because" statements, S1 and S2 | Uses a second code, A to E. |
| C | Best answer, five options | The largest section. |
| D | Budget and cash flow calculations | Data table, then 6 to 8 questions. |

Recent papers hold 120 to 150 questions in three hours. That is about 80 seconds
each. All questions carry equal marks. Do not stall on one item.

Grading: continuous assessment 30 %, end of semester examination 70 %.

## 2. The two answer codes

### Code 1: the i / ii / iii section

**Version A (2005, 2007 and 2008 papers)**

| Write | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | i, ii and iii are correct |
| B | i and ii only |
| C | i and iii only |
| D | i only |
| E | iii only |

**Version B (`2008-paper-student-handwriting.pdf`, questions 51 to 85)**

| Write | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | i only |
| B | ii only |
| C | iii only |
| D | ii and iii only |
| E | i, ii and iii |

**Version C (2020 online).** The options are written out in full. No code.

The two paper versions are almost mirror images. If you memorise the wrong one
you will invert most of your answers.

### Code 2: the "because" section

This code is stable across all papers.

| Write | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | Both TRUE. S2 **is** a correct explanation of S1. |
| B | Both TRUE. S2 is **not** a correct explanation of S1. |
| C | S1 TRUE, S2 FALSE. |
| D | S1 FALSE, S2 TRUE. |
| E | Both FALSE. |

**Method for a "because" item**

1. Judge S1 alone. Write T or F above it.
2. Judge S2 alone. Write T or F above it.
3. Only if both are T, ask: does S2 explain why S1 is true?
4. Pick the letter.

Do not read the pair as one sentence. That is the trap.

## 4. Topic 1: Entrepreneurship and free enterprise

### 4.1 The economists. Learn who said what.

| Person | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Richard Cantillon** (French, of Irish descent) | Gave entrepreneurship a central role in economics. The entrepreneur consciously makes decisions about resource allocation. Pays a certain price for a product and resells at an uncertain price, so assumes the risk of enterprise. Seeks higher yields for money and materials. |
| **Adam Smith** | Spoke of the "enterpriser": undertook the formation of an organisation for commercial purposes; had unusual foresight to recognise potential demand; reacted to economic change and transformed demand into supply. |
| **Jean Baptiste Say** | Combined Cantillon's "economic risk taker" with Smith's "industrial manager". The entrepreneur has certain arts and skills for creating new economic enterprises, plus exceptional insight into society's needs, and fulfils them. The entrepreneur influences society and is influenced by it. |
| **John Stuart Mill** | One of the European economists who shaped the modern idea. |
| **Carl Menger** | Entrepreneurship is the process of converting resources into goods and services of value to consumers. |
| **Joseph Schumpeter** | "Creative destruction": replacing existing products and businesses with new and better ones. Entrepreneurs are innovators. |
| **Peter Drucker** | The entrepreneur uses resources not merely to solve problems but to take advantage of opportunities. Resources are redirected to progressive opportunities. |

**The classic trap.** "Creative destruction" and "entrepreneurs as innovators"
belong to **Schumpeter**, not Menger. When the paper asks what **Carl Menger**
said, the answer is only **iii: converting resources into goods and services of
value to consumers**.

### 4.2 Sources of change that create opportunity

Four sources:

1. Scientific knowledge and the industrial revolution
2. Demographic and market changes
3. Process innovation
4. Social and cultural changes

**Note the split.** The 2005 paper offered these as three items and the answer
was **C (i and iii only)**. In later papers with all three listed cleanly the
answer is **all three**. Read the options as printed.

### 4.3 Distinguishing entrepreneurs from small business persons

Correct markers:

- Vision for growth, and energy to achieve unusual results
- Commitment to constructive change
- Persistence to gather the necessary resources

**Wrong marker: income substitution.** A small business person substitutes
income. An entrepreneur builds growth. Any option containing "income
substitution" is false.

### 4.4 Why small businesses are small

Correct: **by their nature**, and **by the intention of the owner**.
Wrong: "their creation through legal contracts".

### 4.5 Popular myths, and the truth

**The examined version.** "There are several thoughts about entrepreneurs proven
to be more myths than facts. These include ..."

| Statement | Is it a myth? |
|---|---|
| Entrepreneurs "struggle to succeed" and get their ideas by hard work | **No.** This is the truth. The slides say they make their own luck by working hard. |
| Entrepreneurs are mavericks and misfits, and entrepreneurs are born | **Yes.** Slide 2.64 says they are not misfits. Slide 2.66 favours "made". |
| They strike it rich on the first flash of genius, or fail miserably with the first venture | **Yes.** Most ventures start slow and change in steps. |

Answer **ii and iii only**.

| Myth | Reality |
|---|---|
| Entrepreneurs are lucky | Most make their luck by working hard |
| They are "make or break" people | Most ventures start slow and make incremental change |
| They are misfits | They disrupt the status quo. They lead the parade in a new direction. |
| They are born, not made | Environment, background, education, family and careers influence them |
| All you need is money | Most successful companies were founded with little money |

### 4.6 Four main success factors for new ventures

1. A good **entrepreneurial team**
2. A **well planned enterprise** that pursues incremental change and growth
3. Good **timing**, to coincide with the window of opportunity
4. A **business ideology** to serve customers, not to exploit them

Also examinable: the venture's products or services, and markets and timing.

### 4.7 Effects of entrepreneurship on the economy

All three of these are correct:

1. Altering the direction of national economies, and ensuring their stability
2. Introducing quantum leaps in technology, creating new means of production and
   new systems of services
3. Forcing the reallocation of resources from existing uses to new and more
   productive uses

### 4.8 Corporate entrepreneurship (intrapreneurship)

- It helps established companies become more productive.
- It is also called **intrapreneurship**.
- It describes innovation inside established companies through creative
  employees.
- "Intrapreneur" is a contrived word from "**intra-company entrepreneur**".

**To achieve it, corporate managers:**

- combine resources in unusual ways to create innovative products or services
- commit time and energy and risk their careers

**They do NOT take personal investment risks.** The company carries the money
risk. This is the standard trap in this item.

### 4.9 Other recurring facts

- The reason for business failure is most commonly **inexperience**.
- The risk of business failure for a business starting from scratch is
  **higher**.
- Entrepreneurs are **high risk takers**. The slides call them "able to take
  **calculated** risk", so the two readings differ. Answer **high risk takers**.
- Entrepreneurs typically form **service businesses**.
- Entrepreneurs typically have a **high internal locus of control**. True.
- Entrepreneurs **are the life of the party**.
- Not a reason for going into business for yourself: **to live where "I like"**.
- Most technical entrepreneurs start businesses close to their previous career
  work. This is the **corridor principle**.
- Entrepreneurs typically form **service businesses**, not manufacturing ones.

### 4.10 Where the word came from

Slides 1.16 and 1.17. This is now an examined item.

| Statement | True? |
|---|---|
| Derived from the 17th century French **entreprendre**, "to undertake" | **True** |
| Refers to individuals who were "undertakers", **and the term was discovered by business students** | **False.** The first half is right, the second half is not. |
| Refers to people who are **contractors and builders** | **True.** They bore the risk of profit or loss. |

So the answer is **i and iii only**.

A companion item asks what the concept of entrepreneurship has done. It has
**given enterprise a new direction** and it has **been around a very long time**.
It has **not** "just been discovered". Again **i and iii only**.

## 5. Topic 2: Creativity and innovation

### 5.1 The definitions

- **Creativity** is the *ability* to bring something new into existence. It is an
  ability, not an activity.
- **Innovation** is the *process of doing new things*. It is the transformation
  of creative ideas into useful applications.
- **Creativity is a prerequisite for innovation.**
- **Invention** is the verified result of a creative idea.
- **Innovation** is the conversion of something new into useful goods or
  services.
- Innovation is the ability to **see, conceive, and create new and unique
  products, processes, or services**.

> One line memory hook: creativity is the seed, innovation is the process.

### 5.2 The five stages of the creative process

| # | Stage | One word | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Idea germination** | Seeding | The seed of an idea is implanted from curiosity or interest |
| 2 | **Preparation** | Rationalisation | A **conscious search** for knowledge to bring the idea to life |
| 3 | **Incubation** | Fantasising | **Subconscious** assimilation of information. "Sleeping on it." |
| 4 | **Illumination** | Realisation | Recognition of the idea as feasible. The "Oh, I see" moment. A sudden flash. |
| 5 | **Verification** | Validation | **Application or test to prove the idea has value** |

**The stage questions that repeat**

| The stem | The answer |
|---|---|
| A person is in the **preparation** stage when he/she ... | embarks on a **conscious search** for bringing the idea to life |
| A person is in the **verification** stage when he/she ... | **applies or tests to prove that the idea has value** |
| **Verification** is ... | **application or test to prove that an idea has value** |
| A sudden flash that the idea has merit is ... | **illumination** |
| Subconscious assimilation of knowledge is ... | **incubation** |

**Trap.** "Has a sudden flash that the idea has merit" is illumination, not
verification. The 2005 key marked **D (i only)** for the verification item for
exactly this reason.

### 5.3 The innovation process

Elements of the innovation process:

1. **Analytical planning**
2. **Organising resources**
3. **Implementation**
4. **Commercial application**

The 2005 key answer was **C (i and iii only)**: "organising resources and
analytical planning" plus "implementation and commercial application".

**Trap.** Idea germination, preparation, verification and incubation belong to
the **creative** process, not the innovation process. Any option that mixes them
in is wrong.

**The 2007 version of the same question.** "Innovation is the development process
of translating a new idea into a commercial reality and it involves ..."

| Statement | In or out |
|---|---|
| The translation of an idea into a new **invention** | **Out.** Slide 2.40: "Innovation is different from invention." |
| Persistence in analytically working out the details of product design, developing marketing, obtaining finances, planning operations | **In.** This is analytical planning. |
| Obtaining materials and technical manufacturing capabilities, staffing operations, establishing an organisation | **In.** This is organising resources. |

Answer **ii and iii only**. Same logic as the 2005 item, different words.

### 5.4 Windows and corridors

- A **window** is a time horizon during which opportunities exist before
  something else happens to eliminate them.
- When a window opens, competitors rush in and the market saturates. The window
  closes.
- The **corridor principle**: successful ventures evolve from entrepreneurs
  already positioned in similar work or related ventures, so when a window opens
  they move quickly.
- A corridor is a route down which a person travels. One idea leads to revisions
  and further innovations.

### 5.5 Technological innovation: the three key people

**The creative source, the champion, and the sponsor.**

That is the only correct trio. Reject:

- "the inventor, the investor and the champion"
- "the creative source, the entrepreneur and the promoter"

In the 2020 paper the question was reworded as *conditions necessary* and the
answer was still **iii only**.

### 5.5b The seven conditions for technological innovation

Three people **and** seven conditions. The examined statements are:

| Statement | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **A little** support from external sources: government agencies, investors, vendors, suppliers, creditors | **False as printed.** The condition is adequate support, not a little. |
| A clear need for the application by sufficient potential consumers to warrant committing resources | **True** |
| The realisation of the product, process or service as a useful innovation providing value to society | **True** |

Answer **ii and iii only**. Watch the word "little". That single word is the trap.

### 5.5c Major sources of change, the 2007 wording

The slides name five sources: scientific knowledge, process innovations,
industrial changes, market changes and demographic changes.

| Statement | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Industrial changes and **shop changes** | **False.** "Shop changes" is not one of the five. |
| Scientific knowledge and process innovations | **True** |
| Societal changes and socio-cultural changes | **True** |

Answer **ii and iii only**.

> **Method for every "sources of change" item.** Find the phrase that is not on
> the list of five. That statement is the false one. It worked in 2005
> ("industrial revolution") and it works here ("shop changes").

### 5.6 Brainstorming rules

- No criticism is allowed by anyone in the group. Free wheeling is encouraged.
- A **high quantity** of ideas is wanted. Combination and improvement of ideas
  are encouraged.
- The period for generating ideas is predetermined.

**Trap.** "Ideas found without merit are discarded" is **false** during
brainstorming. No idea is judged in the session. The 2005 answer was **C (i and
iii only)**.

### 5.7 Mind map

A **mind map** begins with a person's interest or advocation, expands to include
related products and market opportunities, then selectively follows a path of
investigating each option, expanding new business ideas.

### 5.8 The technology spectrum

- A majority of new products evolve at the end of the technology spectrum as
  **low-tech products**.
- Low-tech products should be easy to make, quick to market, and cheap to
  terminate, **because patents cannot protect them**.
- Differentiating high-tech from mid-tech is largely **a matter of perception**,
  because what is high-tech to one person is mid-tech to another.
- Mid-tech products presume the application of new knowledge. You do **not** need
  to be an inventive genius to pursue them.

## 6. Topic 3: Business planning and the feasibility plan

### 6.1 Who writes the plan

**The entrepreneur.** The entrepreneur has the vision and the motivation and can
articulate the necessary information effectively.

The entrepreneur may consult specialists and other sources. The entrepreneur
does not hand the job to a consultant.

**Trap.** "Feasibility plans are best prepared by specialists **because**
specialists such as financial experts know how to operate businesses." Both
statements are **FALSE**. Answer **E**.

> One compiled answer sheet gives C for this item. The 2005 official marking
> scheme and the 2020 online exam both give **E**. Use **E**.

### 6.2 What a feasibility plan is

A written document that:

1. integrates fundamental areas of business: operations, marketing, finance and
   human resources
2. describes all the relevant external and internal elements involved in starting
   a new venture
3. is prepared by the entrepreneur
4. has the objective of determining whether a new venture can be expected to
   succeed

In the model for developing a new venture, the feasibility plan is **a pragmatic
business plan reflecting the philosophy that entrepreneurs should do the planning
necessary to ensure feasibility without becoming overwhelmed in the process.**

### 6.3 Three perspectives to consider when writing the plan

1. The perspective of the **entrepreneur**
2. The **marketing** perspective
3. The **investor** perspective

**Trap.** In one paper the options were "the government", "all interested
constituents such as employees and suppliers", and "the market and the
investors". Read what is printed. The government alone is not a standard
perspective.

### 6.4 Barriers to success: what to check first

All three are correct:

1. The market, finance, and operations or manufacturing
2. Business concept and the entrepreneurial team
3. Goals and objectives of the venture, finance, and product or service

The 2020 exam confirmed **i, ii and iii are correct**.

### 6.5 The first element of information the entrepreneur needs

**The market potential for the product(s) or service(s).** Only that one.
Answer: **i only**.

Not basic operational needs. Not an evaluation of the team. Not profitability.

### 6.6 The executive summary

Written:

1. to stimulate the interest of the reader
2. in a concise and convincing manner, covering the key points of the plan
3. **after the whole plan is written**
4. on about two or three pages

All are correct. The answer is **all three**.

The **venture definition** in the executive summary names the business and states
why it was founded. The **business concept** in the main body goes into more
detail, including the business evolution and the nature of market demand.

### 6.7 The introductory or cover page

Contains:

1. Name and address of the business, and the amount of financing required
2. A statement of confidentiality of the report, and the names and contacts of
   the entrepreneurs
3. A brief description of the business concept

All three. When the paper asks for a single best answer, the answer is
**a confidentiality clause**.

### 6.8 Common elements of the business plan

The answer depends on which options are printed. Both of these have been marked
correct in different years:

- **Executive Summary** (when the other options are Introduction, Abstract,
  Product design and Literature Review)
- **Market research and analysis** (when the other options are Introduction,
  Abstract, Literature Review and Conclusions and recommendations)

Never pick Abstract, Literature Review, Product design or Conclusions. Those come
from academic reports, not business plans.

### 6.9 Environmental and industry analysis

Deals with all three:

1. Assessment of the location of the venture and its immediate area
2. Assessment of competitive strategies and industry trends
3. Assessment of external uncontrollable variables that may impact the business

The 2020 exam confirmed **i, ii and iii**.

### 6.10 Manufacturing or operations section

Needed for ventures that manufacture, design or sell products, and for service
firms that require capital equipment. It contains:

- **Leadership, the entrepreneurial team**
- **Facilities and inventory**
- **Operations and human resources**

All three. In the older phrasing: *operations, human resources, facilities,
inventory and related issues.*

### 6.10b What feeds the financial section

The financial section is built from the rest of the plan. All three of these
supply it, so the answer is **all three**:

1. The **product and service description**, which sets what is being sold.
2. The **operations or manufacturing segment**, which sets the costs.
3. The **market research and analysis and market plan segments**, which set the
   sales.

### 6.11 What goes in the appendix

**Resumes of partners, facility layouts, and partnership agreements.**

When only one option is allowed, pick **Resumes of partners**.

### 6.12 What the business plan is primarily for

**To give information to lenders and investors.**

Not to guide the entrepreneur. Not to tell suppliers.

A business plan can also **provide direction, prove feasibility, and attract
money**: all of the above.

### 6.13 Why plans go out of date

- **Changes in the market and the industry** (single best answer)
- Loss or addition of key members of the team
- A new location
- Poor articulation of the business concept

### 6.14 Why business plans fail

All three:

1. Goals set by the entrepreneurs are unreasonable and unmeasurable
2. The entrepreneur has not made a total commitment to the business
3. The entrepreneur has no experience in the planned business

When the paper allows only one option, the answer marked in 2020 was
**"Goals set by the entrepreneurs are unreasonable and unmeasurable"**.

### 6.15 Planning is never finished

"Planning is a process that never ends for a new venture **because** businesses
evolve from early start-up to a final growth stage if planning is done well in
the beginning." Answer **A**.

"A good way to fail quickly in a new business is to start without a clear vision
**because** a business without a clear vision shows a lack of understanding of
marketing issues." Answer **A**.

### 6.16 Security of the plan

Many copies circulate. Counter the risk with a **non-disclosure statement**.

## 7. Topic 4: Stages of the venture, and the start-up

### 7.1 The four growth stages

| Stage | What it describes |
|---|---|
| **Pre-start-up** | Assembly of resources. Organising the new venture for opening. |
| **Start-up** | The initial business operations. "Reality shock" sets in as the entrepreneur positions the business to compete in the real world. |
| **Early growth** | The venture has been initially successful and is growing at a healthy rate. Requires careful co-ordination of resources so the entrepreneur gets profit. |
| **Later growth** | The enterprise is established and professionally managed. |

### 7.2 Pre-start-up activities

- **Product/market survey**
- Business concept definition
- A **competitive analysis** is marketing research in the **pre-start-up** stage

### 7.3 Two benchmark considerations for the start-up stage

**Meeting operating objectives and positioning the venture for long-term
growth.**

Confirmed in the 2007, 2015 and 2020 papers. This is a fixed answer.

### 7.4 Start-up operating objectives

**Sales, revenue, growth and position.**

### 7.5 Measuring progress in the start-up stage

**Make a 12 months schedule to see if the plan has been successfully achieved.**

### 7.6 Reducing the financial burden before start-up

**Planning assets carefully.**

Not cutting capacity. Not cutting staff. Not paying low salaries.

### 7.7 Financial planning in the pre-start-up stage

It need not be extensive, but it must be based on **verifiable information**.

## 8. Topic 5: Market research and the marketing plan

### 8.1 Market research versus the market plan

**Market research gathers the information. The market plan then uses it in a
marketing strategy to attract customers.**

This exact sentence has appeared every year. Learn it word for word.

### 8.2 Market research and analysis activities

**Identify potential customers, evaluate markets, analyse competitors, and
describe assumptions.**

Note the fourth item. The distractor drops "describe assumptions". Reject it.

### 8.3 What the market plan answers

**Who is the customer, and where is he/she located?**

### 8.4 The marketing functions

Watch the options.

| If the options include | Answer |
|---|---|
| Product, Price and Distribution, with no promotion listed | **Product, Price and Distribution** |
| Product, Price, Distribution **and Promotion** | **Product, Price, Distribution and Promotion** |
| a. price b. promotion c. distribution d. product e. all of the above | **all of the above** |

The 4 Ps are product, price, place (distribution) and promotion. Pick the fullest
option that is printed.

### 8.5 A market niche

A carefully defined segment of a broader market that defines the
**positioning** of a product or service to create a distinct marketing focus.

"New ventures establish in a market niche **because** companies try to establish
segmentation strategies so that efficient use of resources can be achieved
without ambiguity." Answer **A**.

### 8.6 Results of good market research

A **well defined sales forecast**. Only that one. Answer **i only**.

Not "a good estimate of revenue". Not "well defined investment needs".

### 8.7 Distribution

- A **distribution system** is the **physical process of getting goods to
  market** and the **process of locating services**.
- The types of distribution channels are **consumer, industrial and service**:
  all three.
- The infrastructure of a marketing plan is often built solely on a firm's
  **distribution** system.

### 8.8 Product
An expanded view of a company's product includes **function, form, packaging
characteristics and service after sales support**.

**Product as a catchall term.** A second item asks what "product" covers in
marketing. It covers all three of these, so the answer is **all three**:

1. The **business concept**, such as fast-food franchising.
2. The **physical object or service** sold, with its packaging, image, brand name
   and warranty.
3. The **physical attributes** that shape how customers see it: colours, shapes,
   sizes and materials.

### 8.8b What a marketing strategy provides

As part of the market plan, the marketing strategy gives guidelines on all three
of these. The answer is **all three**:

1. A **marketing program** that addresses the marketing activities.
2. **Expected results**, the allocation of resources, and who is responsible.
3. The ways in which the enterprise will be **controlled**.

### 8.9 Promotion and advertising

- **Advertising** as a start-up seeks to **convince someone to walk in your
  door**. Answer **iii only**.
  It does **not** seek to "make a satisfied customer" or "sell to someone even if
  they cannot afford it".
- **Integrating promotion into the marketing mix** seeks to increase sales, build
  recognition, and build consumer loyalty. **All three.**
- **Integrated Marketing Communications** seeks to **consolidate all
  communications to present a single, focused marketing, sales or image
  message**. Answer **i only**. Not "an assortment of forms to deliver a variety
  of messages": that is the opposite.

### 8.10 The marketing plan

- **Establishes how the entrepreneur will effectively compete and operate in the
  market.** Answer **i only** in the 2005 key.
- A marketing plan is implemented through a **marketing program**. Choose
  "marketing activities" only when "marketing program" is not printed.
- Marketing plans fail because of a lack of a real plan, lack of adequate
  situation analysis, unrealistic goals and unanticipated competitive moves:
  **all of the above**.

### 8.11 Pricing

"Prices for goods and services must coincide with strategies that reinforce an
entrepreneur's business and reputation **because** prices communicate perceptual
messages to consumers." Answer **A**.

### 8.12 Timing

"Market potential is critically influenced by the timing of new products or
services **because** timing pertains to when products are introduced, how they
are priced, how they are distributed and how they are promoted." Answer **A**.

### 8.13 Customer profile

Developing a clear profile of potential customers includes looking at:

1. age and sex
2. income status
3. locating the potential customer base

All three.

### 8.14 Marketing research after opening

Marketing research undertaken **after** a new venture opens its doors is called
**competitive analysis**.

## 9. Topic 6: Intellectual property

### 9.1 Patents

- A **patent** is a grant of a property right by a government to an inventor.
- A **patent search** determines **if an inventor's creation already exists and
  remains actively protected under the law**.
- A **plant patent** is given for **hybrid roses** and **food grains**.
  It is **not** "17 years". Answer: **ii and iii**.
- Requirements for a successful **utility patent**:
  - proving the creation is that of the person making the application

  **ii and iii only**, rejecting "the inventor has taken personal investment

### 10.3 The three major forms of ownership

**Sole proprietorship, partnership, corporation.**

### 10.4 Sole proprietorship

### 10.5 Partnership

Types: **general partnership, limited partnership, master limited partnership
(MLP)**.

- A **general partner** has unlimited liability and manages the firm.
- A **limited partner** invests, has limited liability, and cannot legally help
  manage the company.
- An **MLP** acts and trades like a corporation but is taxed like a partnership,
  so it avoids corporate income tax.

**Advantages**: more financial resources; shared management and pooled
knowledge; longer survival.

**Disadvantages**:

1. Conflict and tension
2. **Unlimited legal liability for all parties, jointly and severally**
3. Division of profits
4. Disagreements among partners
5. Difficult to terminate
6. **The business ends with the death or withdrawal of any partner**

**Trap.** "Limited access to external resources" is **not** a partnership

The 2020 online exam marked **iii only** because its option iii read "unlimited
legal liability for business by all parties, jointly and severally", and its
option i read "complicated form of administration", which is false.

**Advantages of limited partners over general partners:**

1. Exemption from management responsibility
2. Profits and losses passed through to all partners
3. Investment can be sold or assigned

All three.

### 10.6 Corporation

A legal entity with authority to act and have liability separate from its owners.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |

| More money for investment | Initial cost |
| **Limited liability** | Paperwork |
| Size | Two tax returns |
| **Perpetual life** | Size |
| Ease of ownership change | Difficulty of termination |
| Ease of drawing talented employees, through stock options | **Double taxation** |
| Separation of ownership from management | **Expensive to start** (closely held corporation) |

**The corporate hierarchy**

Owners and shareholders elect the **Board of Directors**. The Board hires

The owners have some say in who runs the corporation, but **no control**.

## 11. Topic 8: Financing the new venture

### 11.1 The three capital requirements

| Type | Definition | Financed by |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed capital** | Money to purchase physical facilities: buildings, fixtures, equipment. A long term investment. | Long term credit |
| **Working capital** | Money for day to day operating costs: rent, utility bills, inventories, payroll. | Short term credit |
| **Liquid capital** (fluid capital) | Money held in reserve for emergencies, as cash or disposable securities such as stocks, bonds and certificates of deposit. | Reserve |

These three definitions are asked as three separate questions. Learn all three.

### 11.2 The three types of credit

| Type | Period | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Short term credit** | Less than 1 year | **Working capital.** Self liquidating, since it generates sales cash. |
| **Intermediate term credit** | 1 to 5 years | Smaller fixed capital: fixtures and equipment |
| **Long term credit** | More than 5 years | **Major fixed capital: buildings and land** |

- Lending institutions provide short term loans primarily for **working
  capital**.
- Entrepreneurs use **long-term financing** to purchase major fixed capital
  such as buildings and land.
- If short term financing is used, the funds are repaid from **sales and
  profits**.

### 11.3 Debt versus equity

| | **Debt financing** | **Equity financing** |
|---|---|---|
| Also called | **Asset-based financing** | Ownership financing |
| Collateral | **Required** | **Not required** |
| Investor gets | Interest | **A form of ownership position** |
| Repayment | Principal plus interest | Share of profits, pro rata |

- **Asset-based financing refers to debt financing.**
- **Which does not require collateral and offers the investor some form of
  ownership position? Equity financing.**
- **When interest rates are low, DEBT financing lets the entrepreneur retain a
  larger ownership portion and get a greater return on equity.**
- Long term debt uses **50 to 80 percent** of the asset value as collateral.
- Key factors favouring one type over another: availability of funds, the assets
  of the venture, and prevailing interest rates.

### 11.4 Internal versus external funds

**Internal sources**: profits, sale of assets, reduction in working capital,
extended payment terms, and collecting accounts receivable faster.

**External sources**: personal funds, family and friends, commercial banks,
venture capital, government grants.

**Government grants are an EXTERNAL FUND.**

Evaluate external financing on three bases: the **length of time** the funds are
available, the **cost**, and the **amount of company control lost**.

### 11.5 The sources, ranked

| Source | Key fact |
|---|---|
| **Personal funds** | **The least expensive in terms of cost and control. Absolutely essential to attracting outside funding from banks, private investors and venture capitalists.** |
| Family and friends | The next most common source. Keep the arrangement strictly business and in writing. |
| **Commercial banks** | **By far the most frequently used source of short-term funds when collateral is available.** |
| **Venture capital** | **Intended for higher risks such as start-up situations.** |
| Development capital | For more mature investments |
| Replacement capital | Brings in an institution in place of an original shareholder who wants to realise personal equity |

**Venture capitalists** are investors who look for businesses that offer
extremely high growth and profit potential, have a willingness to exchange
equity, and show potential to cash out in five years.

**Determining which source to use depends on:**

1. the type of financing needed (short, intermediate or long term)
2. the nature and size of the business
3. the entrepreneur's particular financial condition

**It does NOT depend on "money portability".** That is the trick option in the
"except" version of this question.

### 11.6 Terms to know

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Equity** | The ordinary share capital of a business |
| **Net worth** | The amount owners have invested and/or retained from venture operations |
| **Retained earnings** | Accumulated profits in excess of losses and payments to owners |
| **Account payable** | An obligation from the **purchase** of goods or services on credit |
| **Account receivable** | Money **due from** another for services performed or goods sold on credit |
| **Covenants** | Restrictions in loan agreements |
| **Cash flow** | **Receipts minus disbursements** |
| **Financial co-ordination** | The integration and synchronisation of the various financial activities so all functions work towards common objectives |
| **Seed financing** | Needed prior to or during start-up, to underwrite operations, assets or business development |

**Trap.** "Account receivable" and "account payable" are asked back to back.
Receivable is money coming **in**. Payable is money going **out**.

## 12. Topic 9: The financial plan and budgeting

### 12.1 The three levels of financial planning

| Level | Range | Decisions |
|---|---|---|
| **Strategic planning** | Long term | Defining the scope of the venture, which products to develop, **deciding which market niche should be profitable** |
| **Capital budgeting** | Intermediate | **Whether to buy or lease equipment** |
| **Operations budgeting** | Short term | The master budget. Sales targets, production objectives, financing plans. **The most specific.** |

**Budgeting** is the process that involves **co-ordinating the finances of all
areas of the new venture**. Not cost accounting. Not financial accounting.

### 12.2 The master budget

- The three major budget categories are **operating budgets, capital budgets and
  financial statement budgets**.
- The master budget normally covers a **one-year time span**.
- **The budgeting process begins with the preparation of the operating budgets.**
- **The first step in preparing the operating budget is the sales forecast.**
- **Information in the operating budgets is used to prepare the financial
  statement budgets.** The flow is one way. Never the reverse.
- Financial statement budgets are also called **pro forma statements**.
- **Budgeted financial statements prepared from information in the master budget
  refer to pro forma statements.**
- **The master budget is the composition of the numerous separate but
  interdependent departmental budgets** covering sales, production, manufacturing
  expenses and administrative expenses.
- **Perpetual or continuous budgeting uses a 12-month reporting period.** At the
  end of the current month a new month is added to the end.
- **Capital budgets** provide a basis for evaluating expenditures that impact the
  business for **more than one year**.
- A budget is prepared as a planning function, administered as a control
  function, and is a statement of expected results in financial or numerical
  terms: **all the above**.
- Budgeting encourages **planning, co-ordination, performance measurement and
  corrective action**.

> **Careful.** Budgets for expenditure that affects the business for more than
> one year are **capital budgets**. Never "strategic budgets".

The four operating budgets for a retail venture:

1. Sales budget
2. Inventory purchases budget
3. Selling and administrative (S&A) budget
4. Cash budget

### 12.2b Three definitions of a budget that get asked separately

| The question | The answer |
|---|---|
| A statement of **estimated income and expenses** over a specified period of time | **a budget** (not the operating, capital or master budget: the question asks for the general term) |
| Which of the following is a **formal plan expressed in numerical terms** | **a budget** (a financial statement reports what happened; a budget plans what should happen) |
| A budget is | **all of the above**: prepared as a planning function, administered as a control function, and a statement of expected results in financial or numerical terms |
| **Resources** expected to produce current and future benefits | **assets** |

**One weak item.** "Administration plans generally cover a period of" is marked

### 12.3 The pro forma statements

| Statement | What it summarises |
|---|---|
| **Pro forma income statement** | **Projected net profits** calculated from projected revenues and projected costs and expenses |
| **Pro forma balance sheet** | **Projected assets, liabilities and equity** |
| **Pro forma cash flow** | **Projected cash available** = projected cash accumulations minus projected cash disbursements |

- **Before developing the pro forma income statement, prepare the capital and
  operating budgets.**
- **In preparing the pro forma income statement, first develop a sales budget.**
- The main segments of a pro forma balance sheet are **assets, liabilities and
  owners equity**.
- The **income statement** is referred to as a **summary of operations**.

**Trap.** One compiled answer sheet says the pro forma **income** statement

### 12.3b What feeds the pro forma balance sheet

### 13.1 Type 1: Sales budget and schedule of cash receipts

**The set-up.** You get cash sales, sales on account, and total budgeted
collections for October, November and December. You are told the company collects
**100 percent of accounts receivable in the month following the month of sale**.

**The five rules**

1. **Accounts receivable for a month** = the credit sales of the **previous**
   month. That is the money being collected this month.
   For **October**, use the **given beginning balance** on 1 October.
2. **Budgeted cash sales for a month** = the cash sales line for that month. Read
   it straight off the table. This is a free mark.
3. **Total budgeted collections** = this month's cash sales + last month's credit
   sales. Use this to check your work.
4. **Accounts receivable on the fourth quarter pro forma balance sheet** =
   **December's sales on account**. This is the money still uncollected at
   31 December.
5. **Sales revenue on the fourth quarter pro forma income statement** =
   **the sum of the three total budgeted sales figures**.

**Worked example: Topsy Turvy Company Ltd (2007). Beginning AR $40,000.**

| | October | November | December |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash sales | 15,000 | 17,250 | 19,838 |
| Sales on account | 45,000 | 51,750 | 59,513 |
| **Total budgeted sales** | **60,000** | **69,000** | **79,351** |
| Total budgeted collections | 55,000 | 62,250 | 71,588 |

| Question | Working | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| AR for October | given beginning balance | **$40,000** |
| AR for November | October credit sales | **$45,000** |
| AR for December | November credit sales | **$51,750** |
| Cash sales October | read the table | **$15,000** |
| Cash sales November | read the table | **$17,250** |
| Cash sales December | read the table | **$19,838** |
| AR on Q4 balance sheet | December credit sales | **$59,513** |
| Sales revenue on Q4 income statement | 60,000 + 69,000 + 79,351 | **$208,351** |

Check: October collections 15,000 + 40,000 = 55,000. Correct.

**Second worked example: Dandy Electronics (2015). No AR on 1 October.**

| | October | November | December |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash sales | 787,500 | 984,375 | 1,230,469 |
| Sales on account | 337,500 | 421,875 | 527,344 |
| **Total budgeted sales** | **1,125,000** | **1,406,250** | **1,757,813** |
| Total budgeted collections | 787,500 | 1,321,875 | 1,652,344 |

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| AR for November | **GH¢337,500** |
| AR for December | **GH¢421,875** |
| Cash sales October | **GH¢787,500** |

### 13.2 Type 2: Divisional growth to the fourth quarter

**The set-up.** Three divisions or districts. Each has a **first quarter** sales
figure and its own **growth rate per quarter**. You must find fourth quarter
sales.

**The one rule that decides the mark**

> From the first quarter to the fourth quarter there are **THREE** growth steps,
> not four.

**Formula**

```
Fourth quarter sales = First quarter sales x (1 + growth rate)^3
```

**Worked example: GoldCom Corporation (2005)**

| Division | First quarter ($) | Growth | Fourth quarter |
|---|---|---|---|
| East | 520,000 | 2 % | 520,000 x 1.02^3 = **551,828** |
| West | 740,000 | 3 % | 740,000 x 1.03^3 = **808,618** |
| South | 340,000 | 5 % | 340,000 x 1.05^3 = **393,593** |
| | | **Total** | **$1,754,039** |

**Worked example: Hokus Pokus / Akua Antwiwaah Company Ltd (2007, 2008)**

| District | First quarter ($) | Growth | Fourth quarter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kumasi | 650,000 | 4 % | 650,000 x 1.04^3 = **731,162** |
| Accra | 880,000 | 5 % | 880,000 x 1.05^3 = **1,018,710** |
| Cape Coast | 420,000 | 10 % | 420,000 x 1.10^3 = **559,020** |
| | | **Total** | **$2,308,892** |

**Useful multipliers to memorise**

| Rate | Cubed multiplier |
|---|---|
| 2 % | 1.061208 |
| 3 % | 1.092727 |
| 4 % | 1.124864 |
| 5 % | 1.157625 |
| 10 % | 1.331 |

If you raise the multiplier to the fourth power by mistake you will land on the
distractor. That is exactly what the wrong options are for.

### 13.3 Type 3: The cash budget with borrowing and interest

This is the hardest item. It is also the most predictable.

**The set-up.** Three months. A cash cushion the company wants to hold **before
the interest payment**. Interest at a fixed percent per month. Funds are borrowed
and repaid **on the first day of each month**.

**The seven step method**

1. **Beginning cash balance** = last month's **ending cash balance**.
2. **Total cash available** = beginning cash balance + cash receipts.
3. **Total budgeted disbursements** = inventory purchases + selling and
   administrative expenses.
4. **Shortage or (surplus)** = total disbursements minus total cash available.
   A positive number is a **shortage**, so you must borrow.
   A negative number is a **surplus**, so you may repay.
5. **Financing.**
   If there is a **shortage**: `Borrowing = shortage + cash cushion`.
   If there is a **surplus**: `Repayment = surplus - cash cushion`.
6. **Interest** = interest rate x the **cumulative loan balance** after step 5.
   Track the running loan balance. This is where marks are lost.
7. **Ending cash balance** = total cash available - total disbursements
   + borrowing - repayment - interest.

**Worked example: Osagyefo Clothing Company Ltd (2015).**
Cushion GH¢40,000. Interest 5 % per month. Beginning cash GH¢42,500.

| | July | August | September |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginning cash balance | 42,500 | **34,000** | **31,000** |
| Cash receipts | 230,000 | 486,000 | 749,000 |
| **Total cash available** | **272,500** | **520,000** | **780,000** |
| Inventory purchases | 93,000 | 409,100 | 447,250 |
| Selling and admin. expenses | 259,500 | 130,900 | 184,750 |
| **Total disbursements** | **352,500** | **540,000** | **632,000** |
| Shortage / (surplus) | 80,000 | **20,000** | **(148,000)** |
| Cash cushion | 40,000 | 40,000 | 40,000 |
| Borrowing / (repayment) | 120,000 | **60,000** | **(108,000)** |
| Loan balance after | 120,000 | **180,000** | **72,000** |
| Interest at 5 % | (6,000) | **(9,000)** | **(3,600)** |
| **Ending cash balance** | **34,000** | **31,000** | **36,400** |

Answers:

- Total cash available for August: **GH¢520,000**
- Total cash available for September: **GH¢780,000**
- Cash shortage for August: **GH¢20,000**
- Cash surplus for September: **GH¢148,000**
- Amount to be borrowed for August: **GH¢60,000**
- Amount to be repaid for September: **GH¢108,000**
- Total interest for the third quarter: 6,000 + 9,000 + 3,600 = **GH¢18,600**
- Ending cash balance for September: **GH¢36,400**

**Worked example: Birdy-Birdy's Jewellery Shop (2008).**
Cushion GH¢9,000. Interest 2 % per month. Beginning cash GH¢7,500.

| | July | August | September |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginning cash balance | 7,500 | **7,139** | **7,214** |
| Cash receipts | 50,000 | 180,000 | 216,000 |
| **Total cash available** | **57,500** | **187,139** | **223,214** |

| Cash cushion | 9,000 | 9,000 | 9,000 |
| Borrowing / (repayment) | 93,060 | **(3,739)** | **(14,198)** |

## 16. Answers that are commonly got wrong

Learn these as they stand. They are the items students most often lose.

| The question | The answer | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Feasibility plans best prepared by specialists | **both statements false** | The entrepreneur prepares it, in consultation with specialists. |
| The organisational plan describes | **the organogram of the venture** | Not the physical layout, not the form of ownership. |
| Budgets for expenditure impacting more than one year | **capital budgets** | Not "strategic budgets". |
| Asset-based financing | **debt financing** | Not equity. The asset is the collateral. |
| Summarises projected assets, liabilities and equity | **pro forma balance sheet** | Not the income statement. |
| Setting up the specifications of primary clients | **customer scenario** | "Market survey" is the distractor. |
| The planned process of determining prices | **pricing policy** | "Price setting" is the distractor. |
| A marketing plan is implemented through | **a marketing program** | Take "marketing activities" only when "program" is not printed. |
| Entrepreneurs can be described in terms of | experience and family background, personal characteristics and skills, **and** successfully incubating a business | Take the fullest option printed. |

| Lenders' aspects of credit character | **all three**: cash flow, collateral, equity contribution | |

## 17. Seventeen traps that cost marks every year

1. **Answering by letter.** The letters are shuffled every year. Answer by text.
2. **Using the wrong i/ii/iii code.** Read the KEY box first and circle it.
3. **Reading a "because" pair as one sentence.** Judge S1 and S2 separately.
4. **Creative process versus innovation process.** Germination, preparation,
   incubation, illumination and verification are **creative**. Analytical
   planning, organising resources, implementation and commercial application are
   **innovation**.
5. **Illumination versus verification.** Sudden flash is illumination. Test to
   prove value is verification.
6. **Menger versus Schumpeter.** Creative destruction is Schumpeter.
7. **Debt versus equity.** Asset-based is debt. No collateral plus ownership is
   equity. Low interest rates favour **debt**.
8. **Account receivable versus payable.** Receivable is money coming in.
9. **Marketing functions.** Take the option that includes promotion if it is
   printed.
10. **Fourth quarter growth.** Cube the multiplier. Do not raise it to the fourth
    power.
11. **Cumulative loan balance.** Interest is charged on the running balance, not
    on the new borrowing alone.
12. **Cash cushion direction.** Add it when borrowing. Subtract it when repaying.
13. **AR on the year-end balance sheet.** It is December's **credit** sales, not
    December total sales.
14. **Trust the table over the sentence** when a question gives a collection
    percent that the table contradicts.
15. **Intrapreneurs do not take personal investment risk.** The company carries
    it.
16. **Innovation is not invention.** Invention is the verified result of a
    creative idea. Innovation converts something new into useful goods or
    services. Reject any option that calls invention part of the innovation
    process.
17. **One wrong word makes a whole statement false.** "Shop changes", "a little
    support", "discovered by business students". Read every clause of an
    (i) (ii) (iii) statement, not just the first half.
